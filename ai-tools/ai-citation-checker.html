Context

What does an AI citation checker do?

Citation review is one of the more friction-heavy steps in any publishing workflow. Writers paste in references that look right but are structurally inconsistent — missing authors, orphaned inline markers, years that don't match the reference list, URLs that resolve to dead pages. The manual scan takes time no editor has.

This tool runs a structured pass over your text to surface those issues immediately. It maps inline citation patterns against any reference section found, flags duplicate domains that suggest over-reliance on a single source, and checks whether each reference line carries the minimum fields for APA or MLA attribution — author, year, title, source.

For AI-assisted content specifically, the problem is more acute. Language models generate plausible-looking citations that may reference non-existent papers, incorrect page numbers, or authors who never wrote the attributed text. A structural check — even a client-side one — catches the most obvious formatting and completeness failures before they reach publication.

The output is a scored report: citation score, clarity score, and completeness score, with expandable detail sections, domain extraction, and a downloadable plain-text summary you can hand to an editor or attach to a content audit.

Logic

Logic

The tool applies pattern-matching rules across your pasted text to detect inline citation markers, reference list entries, URL and DOI formats, duplicate sources, and structural completeness indicators — entirely client-side, with no data transmitted.

Methodology

Methodology

Citation scoring is derived from a weighted composite of inline marker presence (30%), reference list structure quality (35%), source completeness fields (25%), and domain diversity (10%), calibrated against standard academic and editorial publishing conventions.
